From Medical Shell to Gold Claims: The Nu-Med Plus Transformation

How a cash-strapped micro-cap used preferred stock and voting pacts to pivot across industries—and what it means for the share float.

Research & Analysis — FishyFinance

August 16, 2026

Imagine checking a company's balance sheet and finding just $4,284 in cash. Not four million. Not forty thousand. Four thousand two hundred and eighty-four dollars.

That is roughly enough to buy a reliable used sedan, fund a modest weekend conference trip, or keep a corporate website running for a couple of quarters. It is not, by any stretch of the imagination, the war chest you would expect from a company diving headfirst into the capital-intensive world of gold exploration.

Yet that is precisely where Nu-Med Plus, Inc. (OTCQB: NUMD) found itself.

For years, Nu-Med was known as a micro-cap medical device entity with dormant operations, zero operating revenue, and persistent going-concern warnings. But in the summer of 2026, the company executed a sharp pivot, moving away from its medical-device business toward mineral exploration in Canada.

Corporate reinventions happen all the time in the penny stock universe. But when a company with virtually no money acquires international mining claims and restructures its entire voting architecture overnight, the mechanics are worth a closer look.

Context: The Dormant Shell

Micro-cap public shells rarely vanish quietly; they often hibernate until a new transaction breathes life—or at least activity—back into them. Nu-Med Plus had spent prior reporting periods wrestling with tight liquidity, delayed filing notices, and zero commercial traction.

As of March 31, 2026, the company’s Form 10-Q painted a picture of acute financial distress: current assets barely covered immediate liabilities, and cash reserves sat at a meager $4,284. Operating revenue was non-existent. For an ordinary business, such a position forces an immediate choice between an emergency cash injection or winding down.

Instead, Nu-Med chose a third path: a cross-border acquisition.

The Discovery: Trading Paper for Gold

On June 29, 2026, Nu-Med announced a strategic realignment by entering into a Share Exchange Agreement to acquire 100% of Avid Gold Ltd, a private entity incorporated in the United Kingdom.

Rather than paying cash, Nu-Med issued 4,500,000 shares of Series A Preferred Stock to the stakeholders of Avid Gold. Concurrently, the company entered into a mineral property purchase agreement to secure six gold exploration claims located in Nova Scotia, Canada.

To seal the transformation, the company brought in experienced mining executive Fred Tejada as a Director and Chief Geologist, replacing outgoing director Jeffrey L. Robins. Management also secured affiliate voting agreements to push through a 1-for-27 reverse stock split and expand the pool of authorized common shares up to 500 million.

🐟 Fishy Insight

Preferred Shares and Dilution Overhang

When a cash-poor company buys assets, it often uses equity currency instead of cash. Preferred stock sits higher up in the corporate capital structure than common stock, often carrying special voting rights, dividend preferences, or conversion privileges.

In micro-cap transactions, issuing millions of preferred shares is a double-edged sword. It preserves what little cash is left in the bank, but it creates a potentially significant dilution overhang. If those preferred shares convert into common stock—and if those common shares find their way into the public market via registration rights—the existing public float can get heavily diluted. The pie doesn't get bigger; it just gets sliced into vastly smaller pieces.

Follow the Numbers: The Transaction Mechanics

When tracking a micro-cap transformation, public relations releases often lean heavily on grand visions of mineral wealth. The regulatory filings, however, tell a more grounded financial story.

  • $4,284: The cash balance reported by Nu-Med Plus at the close of Q1 2026, highlighting the severe liquidity deficit heading into the transaction.
  • $100,000: A short-term promissory note obligation owed by Avid Gold that Nu-Med assumed and agreed to repay within 90 days of closing.
  • $45,000: A non-refundable "no-shop fee" collected under the initial letter of intent with Avid Gold. The fee was recognized as income during the period and was significant relative to the company's otherwise limited operating activity, temporarily propping up Q2 2026 net income ($19,670).
  • 4,500,000: The number of Series A Preferred shares issued to acquire Avid Gold.
  • 500,000,000: The ceiling for authorized common shares established under the post-transaction recapitalization plan.

🐟 FishyFinance Lens

And this is where the numbers get interesting.

Nu-Med didn't need much cash to acquire Avid Gold because it wasn't really buying the company with cash. It was using its own equity as the currency.

Nu-Med entered this transaction with roughly four grand in the bank and immediately took on a $100,000 debt obligation. Mineral exploration is a notoriously capital-intensive business requiring heavy outlays for core drilling, assays, and environmental permitting. You cannot fund an active Canadian drilling program with pocket change.

By expanding authorized common shares and issuing preferred equity, management has built a capital-structure mechanism. The assets were acquired using equity currency rather than cash, and future exploration will require additional capital. The real question isn't whether the gold claims exist on paper—it's where that future capital will come from and who ultimately absorbs the cost of drilling them when the treasury is empty.

The Other Side of the Trade

From management's perspective, the transaction offers a strategic path to move Nu-Med into a completely different industry without requiring the company to fund the acquisition entirely with cash.

The legacy medical device business had plateaued into dormancy. By pivoting toward the mining sector, the company has aligned itself with tangible hard-asset exploration targets backed by historical technical reports citing over 600,000 ounces of gold (combined indicated and inferred categories) across its Canadian claims. Furthermore, bringing in seasoned mining professionals like Fred Tejada provides technical leadership for the new venture.

Using preferred stock currency allows the entity to acquire exploration properties while preserving its remaining cash to maintain basic corporate standing.

The Structural Tension

The tension here is not proof of wrongdoing; rather, it is a classic study in micro-cap financial mechanics.

The company's stated corporate strategy calls for active mineral exploration and development, yet its reported working capital is virtually non-existent. Those two facts are individually documented, but together they create an unavoidable financial tension: How does an entity with nominal cash reserves independently finance a capital-intensive mining operation without triggering substantial equity dilution?

What We Know

  • Nu-Med Plus held cash reserves of $4,284 as of March 31, 2026.
  • The company acquired Avid Gold Ltd on June 29, 2026, via the issuance of 4.5 million shares of Series A Preferred Stock.
  • Nu-Med assumed a $100,000 promissory note obligation payable within 90 days.
  • Management secured affiliate voting support to authorize a 1-for-27 reverse stock split and expand authorized common shares to 500 million.

What We Don't Know Yet

  • The exact conversion ratios, liquidation preferences, and anti-dilution protections attached to the 4,500,000 Series A Preferred shares.
  • The independent economic viability and current operational status of the six Nova Scotia mineral claims outside of historical reports.
  • Management's specific, underwritten plan for funding the assumed debt and upcoming exploration expenses.

What Happens Next

  • Filing Reviews: Watch for subsequent SEC disclosures detailing the precise conversion terms of the preferred stock and any newly registered common share offerings.
  • Capital Raises: Monitor whether the company announces private placements, debt conversions, or equity lines of credit to service its short-term obligations.
  • Canadian Registries: Track provincial mining filings in Nova Scotia to confirm active claim maintenance and drill permits under the new corporate ownership.
