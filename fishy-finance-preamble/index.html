Fishy Finance Preamble

Financial markets make information visible.

Prices move. Volumes spike. Companies announce acquisitions. Balance sheets change. Shares are issued. Valuations multiply.

But the most important questions often sit underneath the visible story.

Where did the money come from?

Where did it go?

Who received it?

What changed on the balance sheet?

Who gained control?

And does the underlying business actually support the story being told by the market?

FishyFinance exists to investigate those questions.

Look Beneath the Ticker

A stock price is only the visible layer of a much larger financial system.

Behind a price movement can sit a financing transaction.

Behind that transaction can sit a network of entities.

Behind those entities can sit loans, ownership structures, preferential allotments, related parties, pledges, conversions and other forms of capital movement.

Sometimes these structures are completely legitimate.

Sometimes they deserve a much closer look.

FishyFinance is interested in the space between what a company appears to be and what its financial structure reveals.

We follow that gap.

From News to the Money Trail

Most financial coverage begins with an event.

A company announces a transaction.

A stock rises.

A regulator issues an order.

A company raises capital.

FishyFinance starts there—but does not stop there.

We reconstruct what happened underneath.

Event → Filing → Transaction → Money Movement → Ownership → Market Reaction

The objective is not simply to tell readers what happened.

It is to understand how it happened.

And, where the evidence allows, why the structure matters.

What We Investigate

FishyFinance focuses on situations where the numbers, structure and market narrative deserve a second look.

Corporate Transformations

What happens when a dormant or struggling company suddenly enters a completely different business?

Capital Structures

Who owns the company after a preferential allotment, conversion, restructuring or major share issuance?

Debt & Financing

Where did large loans originate? Who provided them? What happened to the debt afterwards?

Ownership Networks

Who sits behind the entities appearing in major corporate transactions?

Market-Cap Anomalies

What happens when valuation rises dramatically without a corresponding change in the underlying business?

Regulatory Investigations

What can regulatory orders, exchange filings and primary documents reveal that headlines cannot?

Micro-Cap Structures

Small companies can contain extraordinarily complicated financial structures.

That is often where we look hardest.

Follow the Movement

There is a simple idea at the centre of FishyFinance:

Follow the movement.

Money moves.

Shares move.

Debt moves.

Ownership moves.

Control moves.

Sometimes the most revealing part of a corporate story is not the final number.

It is the path taken to get there.

That is why our investigations reconstruct transactions through timelines, money-flow diagrams, ownership maps, capital-structure changes and market data.

The visuals are not decoration.

They are part of the investigation.

Our objective is to make complicated financial machinery understandable without stripping away its complexity.

Evidence Before Narrative

Our core editorial principle is simple:

The story must follow the evidence.

We prioritize the documents closest to the transaction itself:

SEC and exchange filings.

Regulatory orders.

Audited financial statements.

Corporate announcements.

Official registries.

Other primary records.

Secondary reporting can provide context and help identify leads.

But when the stakes are high, important claims should lead back to the underlying evidence.

We don't want readers to simply trust the story.

We want them to be able to trace it.

Fact. Finding. Unknown.

Financial investigations become misleading when fact, allegation and interpretation are mixed together.

FishyFinance keeps those layers visible.

WHAT WE KNOW

Facts supported by primary documents, filings, financial statements or other reliable evidence.

WHAT REGULATORS FOUND

Findings, allegations or conclusions attributed directly to a regulator or investigating authority.

WHAT WE INFER

Analytical conclusions drawn from the available evidence.

WHAT WE DON'T KNOW

Important gaps that remain unresolved.

That distinction matters.

An interim regulatory order is not automatically a final judicial conviction.

A suspicious transaction is not automatically proof of criminal intent.

An unexplained structure is not automatically an illegal one.

Our job is to investigate the structure, show the evidence and make the boundary between fact and inference visible.

Making Financial Complexity Understandable

Financial documents are rarely written for ordinary readers.

A 100-page regulatory order can contain the answer to an important question—buried beneath legal language, transaction tables and hundreds of pages of supporting material.

FishyFinance translates that complexity by connecting the dots.

A multi-crore transaction becomes a money-flow story.

A debt conversion becomes a capital-structure story.

A sudden concentration of shares becomes a control story.

A dramatic valuation increase becomes a market-reality story.

The numbers remain.

We simply make the connections easier to see.

What We Don't Do

No Stock Tips

We are not here to predict the next multibagger or tell readers what to buy.

No Manufactured Hype

We don't turn ordinary corporate activity into extraordinary stories.

No Verdicts Without Evidence

We don't present allegations as convictions or inference as fact.

No Blind Narratives

If the evidence contradicts the initial story, the story changes.

The evidence comes first.

The FishyFinance Question

Every investigation eventually comes back to a few simple questions.

What changed?

When did it change?

Where did the money move?

Who received it?

Who gained control?

Does the operating business support the valuation?

What does the evidence actually prove?

What remains unknown?

These questions form the backbone of our research.

Why This Matters

Not every complicated transaction is suspicious.

Not every rising stock is manipulated.

Not every unusual corporate structure is evidence of wrongdoing.

But unusual structures deserve to be understood before they are celebrated, dismissed or traded.

Because markets can tell you what something is worth.

They don't always tell you why.

That is where the investigation begins.
