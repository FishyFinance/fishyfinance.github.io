What Did Go-Ahead’s Auditor Miss?

The FRC says two Go-Ahead rail subsidiaries wrongfully retained more than £30 million of public money. Its case against Deloitte covers five years of audits.

The £2.4 Million Release

According to the Financial Reporting Council (FRC), London & South Eastern Railway Ltd (LSER), the company behind Southeastern, released £2.4 million in overpayments into profit in its 2016 financial year without informing the Department for Transport (DfT).

Deloitte audited Go-Ahead Group, LSER’s parent, for that year and the four after it. What did the auditor ask about the money, the obligation to repay it and the way it appeared in the accounts?

An Obligation That Stayed in the Accounts

LSER had received erroneous overpayments from the DfT before Deloitte became Go-Ahead’s auditor. The company was obliged to repay the money, recorded accruals against the amounts owed, and began releasing accruals into profit without informing the Department.

An accrual is an accounting entry that recognises an amount owed, even when the cash has not yet been paid. The FRC said LSER accrued a further £27 million from 2016 to 2020 under a subsequent franchise agreement. In its 2019 and 2020 financial statements, the accruals were classified in a way the FRC said disguised their true nature.

The issue was not simply that money had been received in error. Amounts owed to the Department remained in the accounts while some were released into profit.

What Management’s Evidence Suggested

The FRC said evidence obtained from LSER management during later audit years suggested the company intended to release overpayments under the subsequent franchise agreement into profit if the DfT did not discover them. The FRC’s announcement does not say whether that evidence reached Deloitte’s audit team.

A Second Subsidiary

The FRC identified a related issue at London & Birmingham Railway Ltd (LM), another Go-Ahead subsidiary. In its 2020 financial year, after its franchise ended, LM released £5.6 million of accruals to profit and attempted to conceal the release through the wording of a financial statement note, according to the FRC.

The cases involved separate subsidiaries and franchise agreements. The FRC described more than £30 million of public money as wrongfully retained by LSER and LM for an extended period.

How the Department Found Out

In July 2020, the DfT identified an indexation error in the calculation of HS1 track-access charges under LSER’s 2014 franchise agreement. The Department initially believed that profit-sharing arrangements would recover much of the overpayment.

In April 2021, the DfT required payment of amounts it believed were owed and sought a breakdown of creditor balances. In May, LSER told the Department that the overpaid subsidy was higher than the amount the DfT had identified and disclosed additional overpayments relating to HS1 depot rentals. LSER had accrued for both categories because it recognised they would need to be repaid.

LSER paid the principal amounts for the track-access and depot-rental overpayments in May 2021. In September 2021, the government announced that it would take over the Southeastern operation after the DfT identified more than £25 million in taxpayer funding that had not been declared.

By then LSER’s books already carried the liability. The Department was still working out how large it was.

The LSER Money Came Back. The Audit Remained.

The DfT’s annual report said the sums and interest associated with the LSER irregularities it had identified were recovered in full. The £23.5 million penalty was remitted to the Exchequer in May 2022.

The LSER money came back. The FRC’s case was about what the auditors did while it sat in the accounts.

The FRC found that Deloitte had failed to make sufficient enquiries into LSER’s and LM’s actions, apply adequate professional scepticism, evaluate evidence of fraud-risk factors and appreciate the contractual good-faith obligations involved. The FRC described the findings as a “highly concerning pattern of failure” to apply sufficient scrutiny.

The findings covered Deloitte’s audits of Go-Ahead’s consolidated financial statements for the financial years ended in 2016, 2017, 2018, 2019 and 2020. A separate finding concerned Go-Ahead Bayern GmbH, where the FRC said Deloitte did not sufficiently scrutinise changes to the contract-loss forecasts behind a provision.

The FRC fined Deloitte £6.05 million, reduced from £11 million after discounts for exceptional cooperation and admissions with early disposal. It also issued a severe reprimand, declared that the audit reports did not satisfy the relevant requirements, and required Deloitte to report to its FRC supervisor on root causes, remedial action and empirical evidence that the measures taken were effective.
