Travel + Leisure Reported Better Loan Numbers. The SEC Says Cancelled Loans Helped.

Wyndham Destinations reported an improving measure of timeshare loan losses in 2019. The SEC alleges that two undisclosed projects helped produce that improvement by removing delinquent and defaulted loans from the company’s portfolio.

On October 30, 2019, the company reported a Loan Loss Provision Percentage of 20.3% for the third quarter, down from 20.8% a year earlier. The SEC alleges the measure would not have improved year over year without the projects. The loan rescissions at issue ran from May 2019 to at least December 2020, so the total population spans a longer period than the third-quarter comparison.

The number investors were watching

The Loan Loss Provision Percentage measured the provision for loan losses against gross vacation ownership interest sales, net of fee-for-service sales. It was one of the measures investors watched to assess the performance of the company’s timeshare loan portfolio.

By spring 2019, the percentage was moving in the wrong direction. Wyndham Destinations reported 22.5% for the first quarter, up from 20.4% a year earlier. According to the SEC’s complaint, five analysts questioned the increase on the May 1, 2019, first-quarter earnings call. Management said it would stop the year-over-year increases and achieve its 2019 guidance: a full-year percentage comparable to 2018, when it was 20.5%.

Two projects inside the business

The SEC alleges that the company ran two projects to rescind delinquent or defaulted timeshare loans: the Special Handling Project (SHP), which began in May 2019, and the Past-Due Project (PDP), which began in August.

Under the SHP, the company set targets for the number of delinquent or defaulted loans to rescind. Those targets were linked to its Loan Loss Provision Percentage guidance. They appeared in monthly forecasts from May 2019 through late 2020, with senior management agreeing on them at least quarterly.

The SHP focused on loans tied to customers whose earlier requests to rescind their timeshare contracts had been rejected or left unaddressed. The complaint says those requests had typically been rejected because they fell outside the statutory rescission period or because the sale complied with company policy.

The PDP used spreadsheets of delinquent and defaulted loans linked to customer complaints. According to the complaint, the Sales Reversal Committee approved the loans on those agendas in bulk, without reviewing each one individually.

Across both projects, the SEC alleges that more than 2,900 delinquent or defaulted loans were rescinded between May 2019 and at least December 2020. They carried about $77 million in outstanding loan balances, including roughly $34 million in defaulted loans.

How the accounting changed

The complaint says that once a contract was coded as rescinded, the company reversed the original revenue and the related Loan Loss Provision. For loans more than 120 days past due, it also reversed the defaulted amounts.

In effect, the company’s accounting system treated the original sale and resulting loan as though they had never happened.

The SEC alleges that, in several quarters in 2019 and 2020, the projects improved the Loan Loss Provision and Loan Loss Provision Percentage by “more than five percent.” The complaint does not make the basis of that phrase clear enough to translate it into percentage points or a relative percentage.

The complaint also says the company forecast the expected effects of the rescissions but did not track their actual impact on its financial statements.

What investors were told

On the third-quarter 2019 earnings call, company executives attributed improvements in loan-loss measures to operational initiatives, including customer-care efforts, stricter underwriting and changes to the reservation system. The SEC alleges those explanations omitted the contribution of the rescission projects.

For the full year, the company said it had kept the percentage flat year over year and met its guidance. The SEC alleges the undisclosed rescissions helped it achieve that outcome.

The complaint also links the alleged disclosure omissions to capital raising. During the relevant period, the company sold about $1.65 billion of notes in three private offerings that incorporated the filings the SEC alleges were misleading.

The settlement

On September 30, 2026, the SEC announced a settled action against Travel + Leisure Co., the company’s later name. Travel + Leisure consented to a final judgment with a $975,000 civil penalty and a bar on violating specified securities-law provisions, without admitting the allegations, subject to court approval. The company had disclosed the agreement in its second-quarter 2026 Form 10-Q and accrued the $975,000.

The allegations have not been tested at trial.

The SEC’s complaint describes a reported improvement, internal rescission targets and an accounting process that removed loans from the portfolio. The unresolved question is how much each project contributed to the reported measure.
